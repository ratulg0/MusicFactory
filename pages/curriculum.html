The Week

Weekly Teaching Schedule

The academy is designed as a small-group music learning studio — personal attention in every class, inside a collaborative musical environment.

Small by Design

Batch Capacity: 5–6 Students Only

Every batch keeps its strength low — no more than six students — so the music, and each student, actually get attention.

Individual Guidance

The teacher can hear and coach every student within a single class.

Better Discipline

Tiny groups stay focused, practice seriously and grow steadily.

More Performance Opportunities

Every student stays visible — more chances to play, sing and shine.

Stronger Student–Teacher Interaction

Conversation, correction and encouragement flow naturally in a small circle.

The Musician's Batch

One Concept. Every Instrument.

Unlike traditional classes where everyone learns the same instrument, every batch at The Music Factory is a mixed group of singers and instrumentalists, all learning the same musical concept on their own instrument.

A typical batch may include:

1–2 Vocalists 1–2 Guitar Students 1–2 Piano / Keyboard Students

Harmonium students may join the keyboard group when applicable.

This system helps students understand music as a common language — not as separate subjects.

How one musical concept is applied on voice, guitar and piano

Every Month

The Music Circle

At the end of every month, one class becomes a Music Circle — where the room turns into a tiny stage and each student gets to be heard.

Perform for Classmates

Every student takes a turn playing or singing in front of the circle.

Play & Sing Together

Group playing and singing that makes ensemble feel instinctive.

Build Stage Confidence

One small circle at a time, fear fades and confidence grows.

Parents may be invited for selected showcase sessions.

Why It Works

The Learning Philosophy

"Every batch is intentionally mixed."

Because singers and instrumentalists share every class, students learn to listen to each other, develop timing and coordination, experience ensemble music from day one — and grow as complete musicians rather than isolated instrument learners.
